RENEWABLE ENERGY POLICY OUTLOOK — 2026
=======================================
Prepared: February 2026 | Classification: Public

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
2026 marks a pivotal year for renewable energy policy following the passage of the
Clean Power Transition Act (CPTA) in late 2025. Solar and wind capacity additions
are projected to exceed 450 GW globally, a 28% increase over 2025.

KEY POLICY DEVELOPMENTS
1. US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) Phase 2: Treasury finalized transferability
   rules for clean energy tax credits. Corporate offtakers can now more easily
   monetize Section 48E credits, unlocking an estimated $180B in additional
   project financing over 2026-2028.

2. EU Green Deal Industrial Plan: European Commission raised renewable targets to
   50% by 2030. Member states must submit updated National Energy Plans by Q2 2026.

3. China 14th Five-Year Plan execution: China installed 340 GW of new solar in 2025,
   exceeding targets by 40%. Wind additions on track for 120 GW in 2026.

TECHNOLOGY TRENDS
- Utility-scale battery storage (4-hour duration) costs fell below $180/kWh,
  crossing the grid parity threshold for peaker plant replacement.
- Offshore wind: floating offshore costs approaching $90/MWh in European markets.
- Green hydrogen: electrolysis capex declined 35% since 2023; $4/kg target
  achievable in high-irradiance regions by 2027.

INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS
Overweight: US solar developers with IRA credit monetization strategies,
European offshore wind operators, grid infrastructure (transmission, storage).

Risks: Permitting backlogs (US averages 4-7 years for transmission), supply chain
concentration in Chinese solar modules, interest rate sensitivity.

Sources: IEA World Energy Outlook 2025, BloombergNEF, S&P Global Commodity Insights
